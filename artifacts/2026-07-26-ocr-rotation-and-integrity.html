Three revisions, all from one question you asked: is 0/90/270 enough? It wasn't — and chasing the answer turned up a wrong-citation bug on the grounding path that had nothing to do with rotation.

D32

Rotation is a property of glyphs, not pages

The measurement that broke the old design

D31 assumed each sheet has one orientation and picked it by counting label-shaped tokens. To test whether that held, I ran the running header — Sheet 1 of 8 — through all four angles on all eight sheets.

It parsed every time. That single result reframes the problem: the engines already cope with rotated text when it's a confident line. A long string carries enough context to be found however it lies. An isolated 33 on a leader line carries none, so it only reads when it happens to be upright in the raster.

The chooser also picked wrong

On FIG 1 — the one sheet with ground truth, because you confirmed its numerals by eye — token count selected 90°. The truth is 270°. Upside-down digits still look like digits, so counting them can't tell the two apart.

So the fix isn't a better guess at the angle. It's to stop guessing: read at all four, map every box back into page coordinates, and take the union. That raises recall and invites phantoms, so admission is decided by corroboration — a label seen only on a rotated pass needs the specification to recite it, or two engines to agree on the same spot.

Why 180° is held to a stricter bar

Turn a sheet upside down and 6 becomes 9. FIG 4's real 86 manufactured a phantom 98; FIG 5's real 98 manufactured a phantom 86.

Spec recital cannot filter those, because both members of a 6/9 pair are usually recited. That's the generalisable point: against a 68-numeral specification, recital is necessary but weak — it corroborates almost any plausible two-digit artifact. No printed sheet is inverted anyway, so 180° now requires cross-engine agreement.

D33

The caveat has to arrive before the evidence

Where the discrepancies sit in a report

Your framing was that this honours the tool's whole premise, and I think that's exactly right: a system whose claim is ground or abstain can't bury the one step that is neither. OCR is judged. So the discrepancy tally and the caveat now open the report rather than closing it — a caveat found at the bottom is a caveat that didn't work.

One place I deliberately didn't use your wording. "OCR is nondeterministic" is wrong in both directions, and the distinction is worth holding onto:

PropertyStatusWhy
Everything downstream of the readingreproducible frozen into a hashed manifest at ingestion; sheets are never re-OCR'd at answer time
The reading itselfnot guaranteed a different engine version, OS, or machine can resolve the same pixels differently
Absence of a flagnot evidence checks compare drawings against the spec — a numeral OCR missed and the spec never recites is invisible to all of them

That last row is the one I'd want a reviewer to internalise. The counts on the banner are a floor on the discrepancies, not a ceiling.

D34

What the swarm found in our own code

72 agents · 9 lenses · 62 candidates · 43 survived hostile review

The swarm was pointed at anticipating OCR failures. It spent most of its effort reading this repository instead, and the most serious thing it returned wasn't about OCR at all. I reproduced each claim before acting on it.

A distinct part was being asserted as its base numeral

The numeral pattern accepted \d{1,3}[a-z]?. A patent reciting 12A and 12 as different parts had the uppercase suffix fall straight through — 12A matched as bare 12.

This is not an OCR-aid problem. Text is where grounding binds, so that is a wrong citation: a claim about part 12A pointed at part 12's span. It is the one class of defect Cairn exists to make impossible.

The three-digit cap had a quieter failure mode alongside it. The figure-keyed 1000-series (FIG. 10 → 1000, 1010, 1020) is standard in post-2000 software and electronics patents, and none of it was expressible. On such a patent both sides of the drawing/spec reconciliation come back empty — and a coverage report reads perfectly clean over an empty map.

Fixed, and verified as a no-op here

Widened to \d{1,4}[a-zA-Z]?. On US5447630A the recited set went 68 → 68: nothing added, nothing removed. The fix changes what can be expressed without disturbing what already was.

Two integrity holes came with it. image_sha256 was written into every manifest and had exactly one consumer in the whole tree: the line that wrote it. Since every box is stored as a normalised fraction, a re-fetched or re-cropped sheet would still resolve every box — silently, onto a different raster. A confident box over the wrong mark.

And the Tesseract calls never checked returncode or stderr. That one stings: it is lesson L0009, written four milestones ago after a dead engine was mistaken for an engine blind to this corpus — recorded as prose, never installed as code, and rediscovered by a research swarm.

The lesson that generalises

A lesson written down but not enforced by a check gets paid for twice. Two rules now in the library: when writing a lesson, ask what line of code would have caught it and write that in the same change — if the answer is "none", it's an observation, not a mitigation. And a field whose only consumer is its own write site is decoration, not a guarantee.
